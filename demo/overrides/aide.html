Introduction

Roles, access and the end-to-end recruitment process

Cooptly brings the whole recruitment process into one secure interface: receiving applications, booking appointments, scheduling interviews, collecting evaluations and making final decisions.

▶️ A six-step walkthrough appears the first time you sign in. To see it again, open the ☰ menu at the top left and choose Replay the walkthrough.

Roles

RoleWho?What they can do
🟠 Sec-Gen / Board Secretary General, board members (with an account) Set up interviews, open and close recruitment, track candidates, manage the schedule, share links and create invite codes.
🔵 Member / interviewer Members who sit on interviews No account needed. They submit their availability and evaluate candidates through public links shared by the board.

Two ways to schedule interviews

Not every association works the same way. You choose the mode in Settings → Interview rules, and it changes what candidates see:

📅 Candidates pick their own slot
Staff submit their availability before the form opens, and candidates book a free slot directly. Nothing to rearrange afterwards: the schedule fills itself. This is the default mode.
✋ Candidates give their availability
Candidates tick the times they are free, without seeing any slots. Once applications close, you generate the whole schedule in one go from the dashboard. Handy if your staff can't plan ahead.

Doing without staff availability

Getting the whole board to fill in a grid before the form opens isn't always realistic. In Settings → Interview rules, tick “Staff don't need to declare their availability”: the whole board is then treated as free throughout the period, from 8 am until the end time for slots (7 pm by default).

By default, candidates can't book an interview for today or tomorrow: the first day offered is the day after tomorrow. Adjust “Minimum notice before an interview” in the same tab (anything from no notice to one week).

For evening video interviews, set “Slots end at” in the same tab (up to 11 pm): the application form, the staff availability grid and the schedules all extend automatically.

1
Candidates can pick any slot in the period, not just the ones where enough people had declared themselves free.
2
Scheduling works without any availability submitted. Previously, the algorithm refused to schedule anyone and showed “no usable staff availability”.
3
Interviewers are drawn from the staff list (Availability tab), with the same continuity rules. So make sure it's filled in.
💡 It's then up to you to sort things out: the platform assumes everyone can make it and no longer checks. You can adjust panels from the schedule if needed.

What stays the same in both modes: staff submit their availability up front, and interviewers are assigned automatically. The only real difference is who sets the time: the candidate, or the board by generating the whole schedule in one go.

🎙 How interviewers are assigned

Whether candidates book their own slot or you generate the schedule, the rule is the same: the same people cover back-to-back slots. Tying up two members for three hours straight costs an association far less than disturbing six people for an hour each.

1
First, anyone already booked on an overlapping slot is ruled out, break included.
2
Priority goes to those who have just finished the previous slot, so a pair already in the room stays together.
3
Otherwise, the least-used members so far, to spread the load across the whole board.
4
Beyond N interviews in a row, a changeover is forced. Set N in Settings → Back-to-back interviews per interviewer (4 by default, 0 = no limit).
💡 A break in the day breaks the chain: two interviews an hour apart don't count as back-to-back, since the person has had time to leave. And nothing is set in stone: you can change any panel from the schedule.

End-to-end recruitment process

Each association sets up its own recruitment: either over an interview period you define, or on a rolling basis over the coming weeks if you leave the dates blank. In practice:

1
Setup: the Secretary General chooses the mode and the interview rules in Settings (period, length, break, number of interviewers, questions, criteria, rooms), then switches recruitment on.
2
Staff availability: the Secretary General shares the availability link; each member adds their slots there without an account. In “candidates pick their own slot” mode, do this before sharing the form. Without availability, no slots appear.
3
Sharing the form: the Secretary General shares the application link (email, social media, QR code). It's public, no account needed.
4
Applications: depending on the mode, candidates either book their slot (and get a confirmation they can add to their calendar) or submit their availability.
5
Schedule: in “availability” mode, you run schedule generation. In both modes, the Secretary General assigns interviewers and rooms, and schedules any exceptions by hand.
6
Interviews & evaluations: after each interview, interviewers score candidates through the evaluation link (no account needed). The board can see every form.
7
Deliberations: once all interviews are done, the board meets and settles the ranking in deliberation mode.

Form & application

Sharing, self-booking and changing a slot

Sharing the form

At the top right of the Secretary General dashboard, you'll find two actions:

  • 🔗 Copy link: a URL to share by email or messaging app.
  • 📱 QR code: a downloadable image for posters or stories.

The form is fully public (no account required). It shows your association's name and colours, along with the custom questions set up in Settings. The link is permanent (?org=…): there's no need to regenerate it.

Self-booking a slot

Once members have entered their availability, the form automatically shows a list of available slots that candidates can pick from directly.

💡 A slot is only offered if at least N members are free at the same time (N = the number of interviewers set in Settings → Interview rules). Slots already taken by other candidates are greyed out automatically.

After choosing a slot and submitting the form, candidates are taken to a confirmation page that:

  • Summarises the date and time of the interview.
  • Lets them add the appointment to Google Calendar or download an .ics file (Apple Calendar, Outlook).
⚠️ In “candidates pick their own slot” mode, if no member has submitted their availability, there's nothing to book: the form switches to an availability grid and the schedule will have to be built by hand. Remember to share the availability link before the form.

When candidates submit their availability

In “candidates give their availability” mode, the form shows no slots: candidates simply tick the hours they're free during the interview period. No interview is created at this point: schedule generation takes care of that later.

Interviewing together

If your association turns on group interviews (Settings → Interview rules), a candidate who has just booked sees a link to pass on on their confirmation page. A friend who opens it lands on the form with the slot already set: they'll be interviewed together.

💡 Nobody is ever paired up automatically: you need to have received the link. It's designed for two friends who want to go in together, not for grouping strangers.
  • The group takes up just one slot and one panel: two friends together don't take two places in the schedule.
  • You set the maximum number of candidates per interview (2 to 4).
  • If the group is full, the link is no longer valid or the slot has passed, the friend is told and can simply apply as normal.
  • Each candidate keeps their own evaluation form: the ranking stays individual.

Application deadline

You can set a closing date for applications in Settings. After that date, the form shows a closed message. It's optional: without a deadline, the form stays open for as long as recruitment is.

Changing a slot (candidate)

A candidate who wants a different time has two options:

1
Using their personal link, via the “change slot” button on their confirmation or tracking page. The form reopens fully pre-filled, answers included, with their current slot already selected.
2
By re-entering their email address in the form. The platform finds their application and interview. Their answers are kept but not shown again: they don't need to retype anything to change slot, and can re-enter them if they wish.
🔒 Why not show the answers again in the second case? Because an emlyon email address is easy to guess. Without this safeguard, anyone could read a classmate's phone number or cover letter just by typing their name.

Managing candidates

List, filters, statuses and export

The Candidates tab lists every application received. Three views are available from the tabs at the top:

📋 List
Every application with its status, score, notes and availability. Expand an entry to see the full details and change the status.
🏆 Ranking
Automatic sorting by score, or reorder by hand. Set the number of places to see who falls above or below the cut-off line.
📊 Evaluations
At a glance: who has been evaluated, how many interviewers have filled in a form, and direct access to the evaluation form.

Filtering and searching

  • Search bar: filters by first name, surname or email as you type.
  • Status filter: shows only candidates at a given stage.

Candidate statuses

StatusMeaningUpdated by
Received Application submitted, not yet processed. Automatic on submission
Scheduled Interview slot booked or assigned. Self-booking or the schedule
Interviewed Interview done, decision pending or made. Secretary General / automatic after evaluation
ℹ️ The final decision (accepted / rejected) is handled outside the platform and communicated to candidates directly. Use internal notes to keep track of your deliberations.
💡 To change a status: expand the candidate's entry → change the Status drop-down → click Update.

Score and visuals

As soon as a candidate has at least one evaluation, a score ring (0 to 100%) appears on their entry. The colour shows the level: green ≥ 70%, orange ≥ 45%, red < 45%.

Expand the entry to see the breakdown by criterion (progress bars), Yes/No answers with their tallies, and interviewers' free-text comments.

In the Ranking view, candidates are sorted by score automatically. You can:

  • Switch to manual sorting to reorder them as you see fit, by dragging or with the ↑↓ arrows.
  • Enter the number of available places to show a visual cut-off line.

🗳 Deliberation mode

Once all interviews are done, the board meets to settle the ranking. The 🗳 Deliberate button (at the top of the ranking) switches to a full-screen meeting view: the rest of the interface disappears, rows and text get bigger so everyone in the room can read them, and a banner shows at all times how many candidates are in.

1
Enter the number of available places: a cut-off line separates those who are in from those who aren't.
2
The ranking starts from the evaluation score: it's a starting point, not a decision.
3
Grab a row and move it to put someone above someone else. A line shows where the person will land.
4
Every move is saved instantly: you can leave and come back, and the ranking will still be there. Press Esc or ✕ Exit to leave.
💡 ↺ Reset order goes back to sorting by score if you want to start over. The final ranking is also included in the CSV export, in the Rank column.

📄 Viewing an application during deliberation

The ranking only shows the name, score and criteria. To reread an application without leaving the meeting, click a candidate's name (or the 📄 button at the right of their row): a side panel opens with their form answers, the interviewers' free-text answers and their comments.

1
On a large screen, the panel pushes the ranking aside rather than covering it: you can keep reordering while you read.
2
The panel's ↑ ↓ arrows, or your keyboard's, move to the next candidate in the order shown. The 4/46 counter follows the current ranking.
3
The × button closes the panel. The same application is available from the normal list, via the 📄 Application button.

How the score is calculated

Each numeric criterion is converted to 0 to 100%, and the overall score is the average of those percentages. A 1-to-5 scale starts at 0% for a 1 and reaches 100% at 5; a mark out of 20 starts at 0% for a 0. The two types are therefore comparable.

⚠️ No score until the grid is filled in completely. A 5/5 on a single criterion isn't worth 100%: it rests on far less evidence than an average across five criteria, and the two can't be compared. While any box is still empty, the ring stays blank and an orange “2/5 criteria” badge shows what's missing, in the list, the ranking and the application. A Criteria scored column also appears in the CSV export.

Internal notes

Each entry has an Internal notes field, visible only to the board. Useful for recording impressions or decisions during deliberations.

CSV export

The ⬇ Export CSV button generates a file containing every application: identity, status, submission date, interview slot and answers to the custom questions.

Staff availability

Individual entry and combined view
⚠️ Do this first, before sharing the form. Without availability entered, candidates won't see any slots to book.

Submitting availability (via the public link)

Members don't have an account. The Secretary General shares the availability link (the 📅 Availability (staff) button below the dashboard statistics). Each person opens the link and:

1
Picks their name from the list (or adds it if it's missing). No password.
2
Ticks their slots on the grid for the coming weeks (hour by hour, from 8 am until the end time set by the association, 7 pm by default). Click a day or an hour to toggle the whole row or column.
3
Clicks Send. It's reflected immediately in the candidate form and the schedule.
💡 Slots where an interview is already booked are flagged and protected: you'll get a warning before removing them.

Combined view (Secretary General)

On the Schedule page, the 👥 Member availability tab shows a heatmap of the whole staff's availability over the coming weeks:

  • The greener the cell, the more members are free in that slot.
  • The slots with the most availability are highlighted as Best slots.
  • Interviews already scheduled are shown as an overlay.
  • Click a cell (or a Best slots chip) to see who is free in that slot, who isn't, and what's already scheduled there.

This view helps the Secretary General quickly spot the best slots for candidates who need to be scheduled by hand.

Interview schedule

Automatic and manual scheduling, and visualisation

The Schedule page has three tabs: Schedule grid (overview of interviews), Manual allocation and Member availability (heatmap). The grid covers the coming weeks.

💡 In “candidates pick their own slot” mode, interviews schedule themselves. The schedule is then mainly for viewing, assigning interviewers and rooms, and handling exceptions.

⚡ Generating the schedule (“availability” mode)

If your candidates submit their availability, a Generate schedule card appears on the dashboard. One click matches candidates' availability against the staff's and schedules everyone in one go.

The allocation isn't random:

  • Candidates with the least availability are scheduled first: they're the hardest to fit in, and this maximises the number of people scheduled.
  • The interviewer workload is balanced: the least-used member goes first.
  • A room hosts only one interview at a time.
  • A log details the outcome and, for each candidate left unscheduled, the reason (no availability entered, or no slot in common with the staff).
⚠️ Running generation again replaces interviews that haven't happened yet, which is handy when new applications come in. Interviews already held are never touched.

✋ Manual scheduling

1
In the Manual allocation tab, select a candidate from the list on the left.
2
Available slots are shown in green, conflicts in red, and unavailable times in grey.
3
Click a green slot to schedule the candidate. Add a room if needed.
4
To remove an interview: select the candidate and click Unassign. They go back to Received status.

📋 Interview details (pop-up)

Click any occupied block in the grid to open the interview details: candidate information, panel, room and notes. You can edit the room and notes directly from this pop-up.

📤 Exporting candidate links

The 📤 Candidate links button generates a list of every scheduled candidate with their personal confirmation link, handy for notifying them by email.

Candidate evaluations

Interview forms for interviewers

📌 Guidelines shown to interviewers

Your guidelines and question guide appear at the top of every interview form, for the board and for interviewers without an account alike. It's how you remind everyone what not to ask and give them something to fall back on when the conversation dries up.

💡 An interviewer new to the process often has nothing but this page in front of them: what you write here is your only way to brief them. Until something is entered, nothing is shown.

Evaluating a candidate (via the public link)

Interviewers don't have an account. The Secretary General shares the evaluation link (the 🎙 Evaluation link button below the dashboard statistics). Each interviewer opens it and:

1
Picks their name from the list (or adds it). No password.
2
Reads the association's guidelines, shown before they even choose a candidate.
3
Selects the candidate to evaluate.
4
Fills in the criteria set by the board, plus an overall score, an opinion and a comment.
5
Submits. They can then move straight on to another candidate.
ℹ️ Anyone with the link can submit an evaluation, but only signed-in board members can read them: scores and opinions are never exposed.

Viewing evaluations (Secretary General)

In Candidates → List, expand the entry of an evaluated candidate: the Evaluations section shows the average for each criterion (coloured bars), Yes/No answers (✓/✗ tallies), free-text comments and the average overall score. Scores are aggregated across all interviewers.

The 📊 Evaluations tab gives an overview: who has been evaluated, how many interviewers have filled in a form, and direct access to the form to add another.

Settings

Configuring your association's workspace

🟢 Recruitment & interview rules

Each association sets up its own recruitment to match the way it works. In the Campaign tab:

  • Recruitment open / closed: when open, the form accepts applications; when closed, it shows a holding message.
  • Hold interviews: untick this if your association recruits on applications alone. The form then asks for neither a slot nor availability, and all the settings below disappear.
  • Booking mode: who chooses the slot; see the two ways to schedule interviews.
  • Interview start and end dates: the period everything revolves around (staff availability, slots offered, schedule). Leave blank to run on a rolling basis over the next 4 weeks. The two dates go together: one on its own isn't enough.
  • Application deadline: optional closing date. Blank = no deadline.
  • Interview length: in minutes.
  • Break between interviews: an enforced pause between two interviews, so you're not going non-stop. 0 = back to back.
  • Interviewers per interview: the number of members present at the same time (1 to 3). This also sets how many members must be free for a slot to appear on the form.
  • Group interviews: lets a candidate invite a friend to their slot; see interviewing together.
  • Available places: the threshold used for the cut-off line in the ranking and in deliberations.
⚠️ Changing the booking mode mid-recruitment doesn't delete anything, but candidates who went through the old mode won't be converted: those who booked keep their slot, and those who gave their availability wait for schedule generation. It's best to choose the mode before sharing the form.

📝 Form

Add custom questions to the application form:

📝 Text
Short for a single line (city, programme, year group), long for a motivation statement or project idea.
📞 Guided fields
Phone, email, number, date, link. They bring up the right keyboard on mobile.
📋 Drop-down list
A drop-down menu. For campus, team, year of study…
✔️ Checkbox
For a commitment or charter to accept.

Any question can be made mandatory.

Reordering questions

Grab a question by its ⋮⋮ handle on the left and drag it to its new position: a line shows where it will land. The ↑ ↓ arrows do the same one step at a time, and they're what you use on mobile, where dragging doesn't work. The new order is saved instantly and applies to the candidate form.

💡 Reordering doesn't mix up answers already received: each answer stays attached to its question, and a candidate's application shows them in the new order.

📌 Guidelines & question guide

In the Interview tab, there are two free-text areas you can write however you like. They appear at the top of the interview form, for the board and the panel alike.

  • Guidelines: always visible. This is the place for things that are off limits (a question you're not allowed to ask), what to remember to do, and information to keep to hand. An interviewer new to the form should be able to rely on it.
  • Question guide: collapsed by default, opens with a click. Something to fall back on when the questions run dry.
💡 Both are empty to begin with and don't appear on the form at all until you've written something. Nothing is pre-filled: every association has its own rules.

🎙 Evaluation criteria

Define what interviewers will assess during interviews. Criteria can be reordered and deleted. There are two families, and this is the key distinction:

Count towards the score

⭐ Scale
Buttons from 1 to the maximum value, in whole numbers. For a quick gut feeling: Motivation 1→5.
🔢 Mark
A field to fill in, from 0 to the maximum value, half-points allowed. For fine-grained marking: 14.5/20.

Both types are converted to a percentage, then averaged to give the ranking score.

Don't count towards the score

  • ✅ Yes / No: two buttons. Shown as a tally: ✓ 3 · ✗ 1.
  • 📋 Drop-down list: a single answer from your options. Useful for a question that isn't a mark: “Which team would suit them best?”.
  • ☑️ Checkboxes: several possible answers, for example “Strengths observed”.
  • ✔️ Single checkbox: ticked or left blank, for example “Came prepared for the interview”.
  • 💬 Free text: a comment. Every interviewer's answer is kept.

For lists and checkboxes, enter your options one per line (at least two).

💡 On the candidate's entry, these answers are shown as a breakdown: each option with the number of interviewers who chose it, with the most popular highlighted. They're also included in the CSV export.

🏫 Rooms & Bookings

Rooms: add the rooms available for interviews. In the schedule, you can also type in any room name, even if it isn't on this list.

Bookings: set when each room is available. Useful when scheduling interviews, so you don't book a room outside its available times.

🔑 Invite codes

Invite codes let new board members create an account. (Panel members don't have an account: they use the public links.)

  • Format: ASSO1234 (the association's prefix + 4 digits).
  • Each code is tied to a role: Sec-Gen / Board or President.
  • A code can be used by several people.
  • Share it by email or private message; don't make it public.
✅ For members / interviewers, there's nobody to invite: just share the 📅 availability and 🎙 evaluation links from the dashboard. No account is needed.

🏢 Belonging to several associations

A single account can manage several associations: there's no need to create a second account if you sit on two boards.

1
Ask the second association's board for its invite code.
2
☰ menu → Join another association, then enter the code.
3
The ☰ menu now shows a Switch association section so you can move between them.
💡 It's the code that determines the association and your role: you can be President of one and a board member of the other. The two associations' data stays completely separate.

👥 Staff & availability

The Staff tab lists the board's accounts (which you can add or remove), lets you pre-fill the list of names for the public links (availability & evaluation), and lets you copy the availability link.
