First steps

The menu, search, roles and the order of things.

The menu and search

On a computer, the menu is on the left: Dashboard, Candidates, Schedule, Settings. On a narrower screen, it shrinks to a column of icons. On a phone, the pages are in the tabs at the bottom, and everything else is behind More.

At the bottom of the menu, your name opens the account menu: switch association, join another association with a code, light or dark theme, replay the page's tour, change your password, help and sign out.

Search opens from any page with Ctrl K (⌘ K on a Mac), the “Search” field in the menu, or the magnifying glass on a phone. Type a few letters:

  • a candidate opens their record, without leaving the page;
  • a staff member opens their staff member's record;
  • an action takes you to the right place or copies a link: “link”, “stand”, “ranking”, “rooms”, “recycle bin”…

With nothing typed, it suggests the next interviews. Arrow keys to choose, Enter to open, Esc to close.

Hover over a button, a number or a chart bar, and a tooltip tells you what it's for or gives the details.

Each page has a short guided tour the first time you open it. To see it again: account menu, Replay the tour.

Who does what

WhoAccessWhat they do
The board (Secretary General, President)An accountSets up recruitment, follows candidates, keeps the schedule, shares the links, ranks.
Staff membersNo accountGive their availability and evaluate candidates using the links the board sends them.
CandidatesNo accountFill in the form, book their interview and follow their application with their personal link.

A single account can manage several associations: ask for the second one's invite code, then account menu, Join an association. You then switch from one association to the other in the same menu. The code also sets your role, which can differ from one association to the other, and the data stays separate.

How a recruitment runs

  1. Set up in Settings: the period (When?), how interviews are booked (How?), the number of staff members per interview (Who?), the form questions and the evaluation grid.
  2. Collect staff availability with the availability link. If candidates choose their own slot, do this before sharing the form: without availability, they have nothing to book.
  3. Share the form: link, QR code, stand.
  4. Keep track: the dashboard tells you what's left to do, the schedule shows the interviews.
  5. Get evaluations in: staff members score with the evaluation link, or the board does it from the candidate's record.
  6. Deliberate: the ranking, in a full-screen meeting view. The decision is then given outside the platform.

The dashboard

The title is today's date. Just below, four figures: applications, interview booked, interview over and evaluated, with what's missing (no interview, no-shows, no evaluation).

  • Before the first interview: a “Getting started” list in six steps, which tick themselves off (period set, staff added, availability received, form shared…).
  • After that: “To do” (past interviews with no evaluation, rooms missing for tomorrow, staff members with no availability, candidates with no interview, additions to the Messenger group), with a link to the right place. On top of that come inconsistencies: upcoming interviews without enough staff members, a staff member scheduled twice at the same time or outside their availability, an interview in English with no English-speaking staff member, hardly any free slots left while the form is open, a very uneven workload between staff members, a likely duplicate, and impractical settings (interviews longer than the gap between two start times, no evaluation grid, applications open after the last bookable slot…). These last ones also appear at the top of Settings, with a button that takes you to the setting; Settings adds a simple tip when a staff member could do 3 hours or more in a row without a break. An unhelpful alert (a staff member who isn't on campus, for example) can be hidden with the cross, for the whole board; it comes back if someone else is affected, and “Show all again” brings them all back. Then Today, Tomorrow (with the list to copy for the staff) and The next few days.
  • At the top: Stand mode, Share (every link, each with a sentence explaining it) and Copy the form link.

The table of recent candidates has a Messenger column: tick it when someone has been added to the group. The counter and the “Not in Messenger yet” filter track progress, and tick all catches up in one go.

The form

What candidates see, and how they book their interview.

The form always asks for the first name, surname, emlyon email and background (Prépa, AST, ASTi, MSc, MS or Other), at the stand too. Background is the same question for every association: it's what gives the ratios on the dashboard and in deliberation. An old “Background” question on your form is no longer asked (Settings says so); its answers are carried over.

The association's logo (at the top of the menu, on the form and on the public pages) is changed in Settings → Form. It's cropped into a circle: a square image, with the logo well centred, works best. Without a logo, the menu shows an empty circle.

Sharing the links

The dashboard's Share button gathers all the public links. They're permanent: no need to regenerate them.

Booking a slot

Once staff have given their availability, the form shows a calendar: days in columns, hours in rows. A slot only appears if enough staff members are free at the same time (the number set in Settings → Who?); slots already taken show as “Full”.

  • Recommended slots (star): those right next to an interview already booked, or the first one of an empty day, three per day at most. Staff do their interviews back to back instead of having gaps.
  • Fill recommended slots first (Settings → How?): the form then offers only these recommended slots. Every day stays open; only the times are selected, so the days fill up in order. Not at the stand.
  • Minimum notice: by default, no interview today or tomorrow (Settings → When?).

After submitting, a confirmation page sums up the appointment and adds it to Google Calendar or the phone's calendar. The candidate keeps a personal link to the tracking page for their application.

In “the candidate gives their availability” mode, there's no calendar: candidates tick their free hours, and you generate the schedule in one go.

Stand mode

For signing someone up on the spot, on a phone or a tablet. The dashboard's Stand mode button opens it; for staff without an account, send them the “Stand mode” link from the Share panel. Together with the person, you fill in their first name, surname, slot, and the questions chosen in Settings → Form → Stand mode. No minimum notice: you can book for today.

  • The person has nothing to fill in afterwards. At the end, a QR code gives them their interview and lets them add it to their calendar.
  • Interview for two: “+ Add a 2nd person” signs up a friend for the same slot, with the same staff members.
  • People signed up at the stand are marked Stand in the lists.
Stand mode needs an internet connection. If the network is poor, keep a paper list as a backup.

In English

Candidates choose their language with FR / EN: choosing English means choosing an interview in English. The English link in the Share panel opens the form straight in English. Questions are translated in Settings → Form → Form in English; without a translation, a question stays in French.

On the availability page, each staff member ticks their languages. With “Interviews in English: English-speaking staff only” (Settings → Who?), a candidate interviewing in English only sees slots where enough English-speaking staff members are free. Candidates interviewing in English have a 🇬🇧 everywhere.

Group interviews

With group interviews (Settings → How?), a candidate who has just booked gets a link to pass on: the friend who opens it joins their slot. Nobody is ever paired with a stranger. The group takes up only one slot and one team of staff members, but each person keeps their own evaluation.

Changing a slot

  • The candidate: from the button on their confirmation or tracking page, the form reopens pre-filled. Simply by retyping their email, they can also find their application; their answers are kept without being shown again, because an emlyon address is easy to guess.
  • The board: Move, in the candidate's record (next to the interview) or in the interview's details in the schedule. On a computer, you can also drag the interview from one cell to another. See Scheduling someone.

Candidates

The table, the record, no-shows, the ranking.

The Candidates page has two views: the table (one row per candidate: sign-up date, interview, staff members, status, evaluations, score, Messenger checkbox with “tick all”, internal note, link to their tracking page) and the ranking. Search ignores accents; the chips filter (Received, Scheduled, Interview over, No-shows, No interview, Not evaluated, Not in Messenger, English) and clicking a column header sorts.

A candidate's record

Everything is in a single record, the same everywhere (Dashboard, Candidates, ranking, schedule, search). Three tabs:

  • Profile: email, phone, interview (Move, and No-show?, even before the time if the candidate let you know), room and staff members (clicking a name opens their record; Change edits them on the spot, showing who is available at that time), form answers. Edit profile corrects the name, email or an answer.
  • Evaluations: score, average per criterion, staff members' answers and comments. Fill in the interview form opens the evaluation right inside the record.
  • Internal note: visible to the board only, saved when you leave the field. The interview's note, written in the schedule, shows here too.

At the bottom: the status and Delete (to the recycle bin, recoverable for 30 days). ↑ ↓ move to the next candidate, Esc closes.

Statuses and no-shows

StatusWhat it means
ReceivedApplication sent, no interview yet.
ScheduledAn interview is booked.
Interview overThe time has passed. A ✗ in the Eval. column means no evaluation has come in.
No-showThe candidate didn't turn up for this slot.

Reporting a no-show: in the record, No-show? next to the interview; or in the evaluation form (public link included), the “Did not show up” box, which replaces the grid. A candidate who didn't show up no longer counts towards missing evaluations. Cancel removes the mark.

The no-show stays visible after a new slot: “1 no-show” next to the name, in the list as in the ranking, and in the export. It's up to you to take it into account in the ranking: the score isn't changed.

The platform never announces a decision: the candidate's tracking page stops at “Done”. The decision is given outside the platform.

Ranking and deliberation

The ranking shows one short row per candidate: rank, name, a mini bar per criterion, score, and below the name their background and where their interview stands (upcoming date, no-show, not yet evaluated). Above it, a summary: evaluated, past and upcoming interviews, and the breakdown by background of the selected candidates and of everyone. It starts from the score; as soon as you move someone, the order becomes yours and is saved. To move someone:

  • drag the row by its handle;
  • or click the row, then ↑ ↓ (with Shift: five places), Enter for their record;
  • or type a rank in the number box.

Adjusting a score on the spot: on each row, − and + lower or raise the score (by half a point out of 20, or by one percentage point with a numeric grid), for example to separate two tied candidates. The adjustment is kept with the application and shows in the record and the export. The ranking recalculates itself if it follows the scores; if you've moved rows by hand, Re-rank by score takes it into account.

The available places draw a cut-off line. Re-rank by score discards the manual order. Deliberate switches to a full-screen meeting view, in a larger size, with the number of selected candidates always visible and, just below, the breakdown by background of the selected candidates (above the line) and of all candidates, recalculated with every move; clicking a name opens their record on top. The dashboard also shows the breakdown of applications and of evaluated candidates, and the Candidates tab that of the list shown, with a filter and a Background column.

Grey zone

With available places set, the grey zone highlights the last selected candidates and the first ones not selected: the ones you really need to discuss. Set how many ranks on each side of the line (a fifth of the places is suggested, 0 removes it). Show only the grey zone collapses the clear-cut selections and the bottom of the list, so the meeting only talks about the ones in between; “Show all” brings everyone back. During deliberation, the banner counts those still “to discuss”.

Correct for strict or lenient staff

A 14 from a demanding staff member is often worth more than a 16 from a generous one. Correct for strict or lenient staff (below the ranking bar) compares the scores of each staff member's candidates with those of the others. When a staff member's scores are clearly higher, they're counted as more lenient, and the gap is taken off their candidates; it's the other way round for a stricter staff member. The average of all scores doesn't change: only the order moves.

  • See details shows each staff member's gap and the number of scored interviews it's based on.
  • A staff member who has done few interviews is only partly corrected, and no score moves by more than 15 points. You need at least 8 scored candidates to turn it on.
  • The calculation assumes candidates are spread more or less randomly between staff members. If one of them only did the interviews of one day or of one particular group, their gap may come from their candidates, not from them: look at the details before relying on it.
  • The setting applies to the whole board. Hover over a score to see the score from the evaluations and the correction; the candidate's record keeps the raw score.

The CSV export (CSV button) contains everything: identity, status, no-shows, interview, answers, scores per criterion, comments, and the rank in the ranking. When they're in use, it also includes the staff correction, the grey zone and, once the vote is closed, the favourites, the voters who expressed a view, the abstentions and “Widely backed”.

Letting the association's members vote

In the Ranking tab, the Members' vote card lets members vote through a link, without an account: each person ticks the candidates they really want to see join the association (their “favourites”).

  • Set up a vote: choose the number of favourites per person (a third of the places is suggested) and who votes (the staff list, to which you can add names). Only these names can vote. Voters see the candidates' name and background, not their score.
  • Copy link, then send it to the members. It's only offered in this tab. Each person picks their name, ticks, sends. Several people can vote one after another on the same phone: nothing is kept, and picking a name never shows that person's previous vote.
  • Abstain: a close friend, a flatmate? The Abstentions button on the voting page lets you abstain on up to 5 candidates. The vote then counts neither for nor against them.
  • The dashboard (To do) tells you who hasn't voted yet, so you can chase them up, then when everyone has voted.
  • Voting again replaces the previous vote: only the last one counts. If the same name has voted several times, the card says so (“Camille (2)”): if it's abuse, delete the vote and start again.
  • Votes stay secret until the vote is closed, for the board too: the card only shows who has voted. Close the vote closes the link and shows the favourites received on each row of the ranking; you can still Reopen the vote.
  • Once the vote is closed, each row of the ranking shows the favourites received and the abstentions. Widely backed marks those who are a favourite of at least two thirds of voters (not counting those who abstained on them).
  • Rank by votes puts the highest share of favourites first, among the voters who didn't abstain, then uses the score to break ties. Then adjust as usual (move, − / +).
  • Delete the vote erases the vote, all the ballots and the link; the ranking doesn't change. Closing the recruitment also erases votes.

The score

Each numeric criterion is converted to 0 to 100%, and the score is the average of these percentages: a 1-to-5 scale and a mark out of 20 can be compared. Green from 70%, orange from 45%, red below.

No score until the grid is complete: a 5/5 on a single criterion isn't worth 100%. A “2/5 criteria” badge says what's missing.

Staff

Availability, a staff member's record, choosing the staff members for each interview.

Availability

Staff members don't have an account. Send them the availability link (Share panel). Each person picks their name, ticks their hours over the period and their languages, and sends. It's taken into account straight away, in the form as in the schedule.

  • A second click turns a cell orange, “only if there's no other option”: usable, but never recommended, and these staff members are only picked as a last resort.
  • An hour where the staff member already has an interview is protected: you're warned before removing it.

No time to get availability filled in? In Settings → Who?, “Staff don't need to declare their availability”: everyone is treated as free throughout the period, and it's up to you to sort things out afterwards.

A staff member's record

Clicking a staff member's name (a candidate's record, an interview's details, search) opens their record: their availability over the period (available, last resort, in an interview), their interviews with ✓ or ✗ depending on the evaluation, and their languages. Edit their availability corrects it on their behalf, from the schedule.

Who runs the interview

Whether candidates book themselves or you generate the schedule, staff members are chosen by the same rule: the same people cover consecutive slots. Bringing in two people for three hours costs less than disturbing six for an hour each.

  1. Anyone already busy at that time is ruled out, break included.
  2. Priority goes to those who have just run the interview right before.
  3. Otherwise, the least busy so far.
  4. Beyond N interviews in a row, a changeover is forced (Settings → Who?, 4 by default, 0 = no limit).

A break in the day breaks the chain. And nothing is set in stone: you can change the staff members of any interview from the schedule.

The schedule

See interviews and availability, schedule someone, spread the staff.

One view, three displays

At the top of the grid:

  • Interviews / Availability / Both: availability colours the grid; the darker the cell, the more staff members are free, with their number in each cell.
  • Day / Week / All, with the arrows to move forward; clicking the date takes you back to today. On a phone, one day at a time.
  • Search (candidate, staff member, room) and the All staff filter, to see only one person's interviews.

An interview's colour: green, complete; orange, room to be set; red, staff members to be set. Clicking the date of a column copies the day's list, ready to paste for the staff. Clicking an interview opens its details: room, interview in English, staff members (🇬🇧 = speaks English), private note, and the Candidate tab. Every change saves itself; at the bottom, Delete and Move.

Below the grid, Interviews per staff member gives each person's workload. The public schedule link lets staff see the schedule and add their interviews to their calendar.

Scheduling someone

Clicking a free cell shows the staff members free at that time, the no-shows to reschedule (candidates marked as no-shows: “Reschedule here” moves their interview, and the no-show stays on record) and the candidates with no interview (those available at that time first). Schedule here opens a confirmation with staff members already suggested: adjust if needed, add the room, confirm.

Moving an interview (the Move button in its details, or dragging it on a computer): a banner appears at the top of the schedule and each cell shows whether it works.

  • Light cell: enough staff members free. Not necessarily the same ones as before: they're suggested automatically, the previous ones first if they're free.
  • ⭐: the cell sits right next to an interview already planned, so the staff carry on instead of waiting (same rule as the stars on the form).
  • Striped cell: staff members are missing, or the limit of simultaneous interviews has been reached. You can still choose it.
  • A green dot: the candidate said they were available at that time (“the candidate gives their availability” mode).

Click the cell: a confirmation shows the old and new times, the staff members and the room. A group interview (candidates interviewed together) moves as a whole; untick to move only the chosen person. Afterwards, a ready-to-copy message lets you tell the candidate (their tracking page already shows the new time). Cancel or Esc leaves the choice.

The Schedule a candidate tab lists the candidates with no interview, those to reschedule and the others: a click opens the same choice in the schedule.

The Availability by person tab shows the map of everyone's availability, or one person's, with Edit their availability. At the top, Copy the availability link to send to the staff again; at the bottom, Availability per staff member: hours declared over the period (including “last resort”, and what's still to come), planned interviews and languages, to see whom to chase up.

Optimise

The Optimise button reassigns the staff members of interviews from tomorrow onwards to avoid bringing someone in for a single interview, cut the waiting between two interviews and balance the workload.

  • It never changes the time of an interview, only the staff members.
  • It respects availability, English and the number of interviews in a row.
  • It shows before and after, then the list of changes: untick the ones you don't want, then Apply.
  • If there's nothing to improve, it says so.
Remember to let the staff members concerned know: the public schedule is updated straight away.

Generating the schedule

In “the candidate gives their availability” mode, the dashboard offers Generate schedule: it matches the candidates' availability against the staff's and schedules everyone in one go, the least available candidates first. A log gives the reason for each candidate left unscheduled. Running it again replaces upcoming interviews; those already held never move.

Evaluations

Scoring a candidate, with or without an account.

Staff members use the evaluation link: they choose the candidate (only interviews around now are offered, adjustable in Settings → Evaluation), read their answers and your guidelines, fill in the grid, and send. If the person didn't come, the “Did not show up” box reports the no-show to the board.

The board evaluates from the candidate's record, Evaluations tab, Fill in the interview form. The same no-show box is there too.

Evaluations can only be read by the board. To see who hasn't been evaluated yet: the Not evaluated chip in Candidates, or the “Past interviews with no evaluation” line on the dashboard.

Guidelines and question guide (Settings → Evaluation) appear at the top of every interview form, including for staff members without an account: that's the place for questions not to ask.

Settings

One tab per question. Everything saves itself.

Settings are arranged by question: When? How? Who?, then Form, Evaluation, Access and security. There's no Save button: every change goes through by itself, and the time of the last save shows at the top. Each block has an “In plain English” summary that spells out what your settings produce, for example the list of times candidates will be able to choose. The Find a setting field takes you to the right place (“English”, “room”, “notice”).

When?

  • Recruitment open or paused: when paused, the form shows a holding message.
  • Interview start and end: left blank, it's the next four weeks. The two dates go together. Application deadline: optional.
  • Interview days: untick days without interviews (weekends) and remove dates (bank holiday, exams). An interview already set on that day stays.
  • Length, break between two interviews, slots end (up to 11 pm for evening video interviews), offered slots (every quarter of an hour, half hour or hour) and minimum notice before an interview.

How?

  • Organise interviews: untick to recruit on applications alone.
  • The candidate picks their slot (the default, the schedule fills itself) or the candidate gives their availability (you generate the schedule afterwards). Best to choose before sharing the form.
  • Group interviews, Fill recommended slots first, flats as a venue, and simultaneous interviews (maximum), for example the number of rooms. In the schedule, you can always schedule someone beyond that by hand.
  • Rooms and their bookings. In the schedule, you can also type a room that isn't on the list.

Who?

  • Staff members per interview (minimum), from 1 to 3: that many must be free for a slot to exist.
  • An extra staff member when someone is free: for example “2 minimum, up to 3”, only if it doesn't cost any other interview.
  • Back-to-back interviews per staff member, interviews in English with English-speaking staff, and staff don't need to declare their availability.
  • Staff members (board accounts and their role) and availability without an account: the list of names offered on the availability and evaluation links.

Form

Your questions: short or long text, phone, email, number, date, link (these bring up the right keyboard on a phone), drop-down list, checkbox. Each one can be required and translated into English. To reorder them, drag them by their handle or use the arrows: answers already received stay attached to their question. The Stand mode block chooses what's asked on the spot.

Evaluation

  • Staff evaluation link: interviews from 2 hours before to 2 hours after now, or the whole day's.
  • Guidelines (always visible) and question guide (collapsed), empty to begin with.
  • Criteria: the scale (buttons 1 to N) and the mark (a field, half-points accepted) count towards the score; yes / no, list, checkboxes, single checkbox and free text don't count, they're shown as a tally in the record.
  • Available places: the ranking's cut-off line.

Access and security

  • Invite codes: so new board members can create an account (the association's prefix and four digits, with a role). Staff members don't need one. Don't make them public.
  • Recycle bin: everything the board deletes stays there for 30 days, with Restore.
  • Danger zone: bulk deletions, after confirming with the association's name. They also go through the recycle bin.

End of recruitment

Closing, erasing, and handing over to the next board.

The next board is often recruited from this year's candidates: they mustn't see how they were scored. So when you close, all candidate data is erased, selected candidates included. All that remains are anonymous figures per year (applications, interviews, evaluated, no-shows), and everything the board has set up.

Closing

  1. When all interviews are over and the ranking is settled, download the ranking: Candidates page, CSV button.
  2. Settings → When?, End of recruitment block: Close the recruitment. The form closes straight away.
  3. Candidate data (answers, interviews, evaluations, notes, no-shows, staff availability, recycle bin) is erased 30 days later, permanently. Until then, you can reopen in case of a mistake, or erase now.

The dashboard reminds you to close once the period is over, and shows the erasure date once it's closed. Erasure happens the first time the dashboard is opened after the planned date.

Preparing the next one

Prepare the next recruitment (same block) erases the data if that hasn't been done yet, removes last year's dates and keeps the rest: settings, questions, evaluation grid, guidelines, rooms. The next time the dashboard is opened, the setup assistant offers to set everything up in five screens: dates, interviews, staff members, appointments, form. Each screen starts from last year's choices; you can open recruitment at the end, or later.

Handing over

Settings → Access and security, Board handover:

  1. Create the new board's code, and copy the ready-made message to send them: each person creates their account with it.
  2. Once the new board is in, remove the old one: tick those who are leaving. You can't remove yourself: the new board does it.
  3. Clear the staff list, so the new staff sign up on the availability and evaluation links.
If nobody from the old board is available, the platform administrator can do all these steps on their behalf.

Frequently asked questions

The most common cases.

The form doesn't offer any slots
There are four possible causes. The mode is “the candidate gives their availability”: that's expected. No staff member has given their availability: send the availability link. Not enough staff members are free at the same time for the number required per interview: ask a few of them to widen their availability, or lower the number in the Who? tab of Settings. The period is over or set incorrectly: check the When? tab of Settings.
A candidate didn't show up
Open their record and click No-show? next to the interview, or tick “Did not show up” in the evaluation form. To give them a second chance: Move to choose the time yourself, or Their link to send them their personal link, where they choose a new slot themselves (the form is already filled in). Either way, the no-show stays on record, visible in the ranking.
The schedule brings staff members in for a single interview
Click Optimise in the schedule: it suggests new staff members for interviews from tomorrow onwards, without touching the times, and you approve each change. For the future, the Fill recommended slots first option (Settings → How?) groups bookings together.
How do I schedule someone by hand?
In the schedule, click a free cell, then Schedule here next to the candidate. Staff members are suggested: all that's left is the room and confirming. Or, from their record, Schedule: the schedule opens and you choose the cell.
How do I delete an interview?
In the schedule, click the interview, then Delete at the bottom of its details. It goes to the recycle bin, recoverable for 30 days.
I deleted a candidate by mistake
Settings → Access and security, Recycle bin, then Restore: their interview, evaluations, notes and no-shows come back with them. After 30 days, the item is erased. For an erasure request, use Delete permanently.
How do I remind staff which questions not to ask?
Write them in Settings → Evaluation, Guidelines. They appear at the top of every interview form, including through the public link, which is often the only page the staff member has in front of them.
Which booking mode should I choose?
If staff can give their availability before the form opens, go for “the candidate picks their slot”: the schedule fills itself. Otherwise, choose “the candidate gives their availability”, and generate the schedule at the end.
How do I pause recruitment?
Settings → When?, the Recruitment open switch. The form then shows a holding message.
Recruitment is over: what now?
Download the ranking (Candidates, CSV button), then Settings → When?, Close the recruitment. Candidate data will be erased 30 days later. For what comes next, see End of recruitment.
Why is there a watermark on the pages?
A discreet watermark (email and date) shows up in screenshots. It makes it possible to trace the source of a leak and protects candidates' personal data.
